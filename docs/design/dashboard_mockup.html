What counts as evidence

We only call something a lead when it passes all of these. Otherwise the verdict is "no head start", which is a real answer and not a failure.

Counts

  • The odds change before the stock, again and again, not just once.
  • Knowing the odds helps predict the stock even after we account for the stock's own recent moves.
  • The pattern disappears when we deliberately scramble the timing. If it doesn't, the method is fooling us.
  • It holds up even though we tested many pairs. If you test enough things, one of them will look good by luck.

Doesn't count

  • Two lines that look alike on a chart.
  • One dramatic day.
  • Both moving in the same hour. That just means both reacted to the same news.
  • A link that shows up in one hand-picked stock but not in the others.

"No head start" is a result too

If the odds only mirror what stocks are already doing, that is worth knowing. It means you can ignore them for timing and skip the effort. Proving there's nothing there is half of what this lab is for.

    Plain words used here, and the technical terms behind them
    Odds
    Polymarket's price for a yes/no outcome, read as a crowd-estimated probability. analysis uses hourly changes in log-odds
    Who moves first
    Comparing the two series at time gaps from 12 hours one way to 12 hours the other. lead-lag cross-correlation, lags −12…+12 h
    Link strength
    From −1 to 1. Zero means no link. In markets, 0.1 to 0.2 is already notable. Pearson correlation
    Noise zone
    The range that pure chance would produce. 95% band under no relationship
    Big odds jump
    An hour in which the odds moved far more than usual. > 2σ hourly change
    Shaded range
    Where the true average plausibly lies. If it includes zero, the effect could be chance. 95% confidence interval
    Helps predict beyond its own past
    Do the odds add anything once we know the stock's recent moves? Granger causality test
    Scrambled-timing test
    Rerun with shuffled times. A real signal should vanish. placebo test
    Many-pairs correction
    Raises the bar because we test many pairs at once. Benjamini–Hochberg FDR adjustment
    Control asset
    A stock that should have no link, used to check that the method isn't inventing patterns. negative control
    Hourly data, all times UTC, 45-day window. Research only: this lab places no trades and is not investment advice.
